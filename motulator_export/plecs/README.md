# PLECS export

Export of *motulator* systems to PLECS Standalone models (`write_model` and `simulate` of `motulator_export.plecs`).
The system model is built from the native blocks of PLECS, and the control system runs the C port of the *motulator* control algorithms (`../c`) in C-Script blocks.
The control systems are described as netlists, which the Simulink export renders too (see [the Simulink export](../simulink/README.md)).
The parts shared with the Simulink export and the native simulation, e.g., the step signals, the gate enable, and the checks of the PWM, are in `../_common.py`.
The example scripts are in [examples/](../../examples/README.md).

## Structure of the models

- The references (e.g., the speed reference or the power references) and the load torque are Step blocks; several steps are given as vector parameters of one Step block and summed by a Gain block. A constant reference (the converter voltage reference of grid-forming control) is a Constant block.
- The first input of the control system is `enable`, the gate enable of the control system of *motulator* (`ctrl.set_enable`): a Constant block of 1 by default, or a Step block. While it is zero, the gates are blocked, and the control system runs in standby as in *motulator*, without measuring the converter voltage: the observers of the drives run on their open-circuit model and the controller computes its output for zero torque, the grid converters use the measured PCC voltage as the converter voltage, and the speed controller is not updated. When the gates are released, a sensorless drive catches the rotating machine with a flying start, as in *motulator*: the observer is initialized from the current response to a zero-voltage period (the block `standby` and the input `standby` of the PWM, the speed controller, and `modulate`). The tag `enable` also takes the input to the gates of the converter (see below). The control system limits the measured DC-bus voltage to at least 1 V.
- The control system contains a block for each class of *motulator*, in the hierarchy of *motulator*. Each block of a class is a masked subsystem with the arguments of its class, containing a C-Script block that runs the C code of the class:
  - Flux-vector control: `VectorControlSystem` (the masked subsystem) contains the measurements (`get_measurement`), the `PWM` (a subsystem with `compute_output` and `get_realized_voltage`), the `SpeedController`, the `FluxVectorController`, `modulate` (the voltage reference in stationary coordinates, its angle advanced by `k_comp*T_s*w_c` with `k_comp = 1.5` of the controller, or 0 with the direct discrete-time current controller), and the monitored signals. The `FluxVectorController` contains the `ReferenceGenerator`, the `FluxTorqueController`, and the `SpeedFluxObserver`, which contains the `SpeedObserver` and the `FluxObserver`; that of an induction machine contains the `ReferenceGenerator` and the `FluxTorqueController` (with the integral action) of its V/Hz control and the `SpeedFluxObserver` of its current-vector control.
  - Current-vector control: the `CurrentVectorController` contains the `CurrentReferenceGenerator` (of an induction machine) or the `ReferenceGenerator` with the current reference (of a synchronous machine, the observer and the reference generator being those of flux-vector control), the `CurrentController`, and the `SpeedFluxObserver`. When the observer of a synchronous machine catches the machine after the release of the gates, the current reference and the current controller restart from the zero-torque operating point of the caught speed. With `cfg.discrete`, the `CurrentController` is the direct discrete-time controller (`DiscreteCurrentController`), which updates its state with the limited voltage reference of the PWM (the output `u_c_lim_ab` of the `PWM`, excluding the compensation of the duty-ratio error, while the gates are enabled), and the mask initialization of the control system sets `k_comp = 0`, since the design includes the delays.
  - Torque-control mode (`VectorControlSystem` without the speed controller, `set_torque_ref`): the control systems of the vector controllers have their own blocks (e.g., `Flux-vector control (torque)`), whose second input is the torque reference, which the block `torque_reference` gives to the vector controller (zero in standby), and whose mask has no `speed_ctrl`; the monitored signals are those of the speed control without the speed reference.
  - V/Hz control: `VHzControlSystem` contains the `RateLimiter` of the speed reference instead of the speed controller, and the `ObserverBasedVHzController` contains the `FluxObserver` (without speed estimation, the rotor speed being the rate-limited speed reference), the `ReferenceGenerator`, the `FluxTorqueController`, and the low-pass filter of the torque estimate (`tau_M_lpf`). For an induction machine, pure open-loop V/Hz control is the special case `par.L_M = inf`, for which the mask initialization of the `ObserverBasedVHzController` sets the unit observer gain and the other settings, as *motulator* does. For a synchronous machine, the `ReferenceGenerator` and the `FluxTorqueController` (without the integral action) are those of flux-vector control, and the mask initialization of the `ObserverBasedVHzController` resolves the defaults of `create_vhz_observer`.
  - Grid-following control: the `CurrentVectorController` of `GridConverterControlSystem` contains the `PLL`, the `CurrentController`, and its `compute_output` before and after the `CurrentLimiter` (the current reference from the power references, and the power references corresponding to the limited current reference, `power_reference`).
  - Grid-forming control: the `ObserverBasedGridFormingController` contains the `Observer` (designed in discrete time), the `PowerReferenceLimiter` (no limitation without `cfg.i_d_max`), and its `compute_output` before and after the `CurrentLimiter` (the current reference from the limited active-power reference, and the voltage reference).
- The masks of the composite classes pass the arguments to the blocks inside them as the constructors of *motulator*, e.g., the mask initialization of the `FluxVectorController` resolves the defaults of `FluxVectorController`, and that of the `SpeedFluxObserver` resolves the defaults of `create_speed_flux_observer` and computes the gains of `SpeedFluxObserver` (with the speed estimation poles matched to the sampling period).
- The mask of the control system has the arguments of its top-level class as structs defined in the initialization commands of the model, which read as the *motulator* example (`[]` is `None`): `vector_ctrl` (the vector controller, with its machine model `par`, its configuration `cfg`, and the initial state of its observer `observer`, i.e., the initial speed estimate `observer.w_M` of the `SpeedFluxObserver`, which *motulator* sets as `vector_ctrl.observer.w_M`, and which the mask initialization sets to zero if it is missing, e.g., in a model written before it was exported), `speed_ctrl` (`SpeedController`), and `pwm` (the PWM) of `VectorControlSystem`, `vhz_ctrl`, `slew_rate`, and `pwm` of `VHzControlSystem`, and `inner_ctrl` and `pwm` of `GridConverterControlSystem`. The mask passes them to the blocks inside it as their parameters, since the blocks inside a masked subsystem of PLECS see only the variables of its mask. Its mask initialization gives the sampling period `T_s` from the configuration of the inner controller, and the delay and the PWM of the model use `cfg.T_s` of the initialization commands.
- The measurements, the feedback signals, and the references are signal vectors (`meas`, `fbk`, and `ref`, defined in the C headers, e.g., `../c/sm_flux_vector.h`), whose fields are selected by Signal Selector blocks (e.g., `fbk.w_M`) where *motulator* accesses them. The input `enable` is distributed by Goto and From blocks, as are the feedback signals and the references to the speed controller and to the monitored signals, the voltage reference fed back to the field weakening and to the active-power limitation, and the current to the active-power limitation.
- The control system samples the references and the measurements (e.g., the phase currents, the DC-bus voltage, and the rotor angle or speed in the sensored mode) with the sampling period `T_s`. The duty ratios pass through a Delay block, which models the computational delay of one sampling period, as in *motulator*.
- The Symmetrical PWM block of PLECS (regular sampling with double update, carrier frequency `1/(2*T_s)`) generates the gate signals of the IGBT converter of PLECS with ideal IGBTs and diodes, which are multiplied by the gate enable sampled at the sampling instants (a Zero-Order Hold block), as the control system of *motulator* evaluates the gate enable. While it is zero, the gate signals are zero, i.e., the switches are off, and the current directions determine the conducting diodes, as in *motulator*.
- The PWM corresponds to carrier comparison in *motulator* (`pwm=True`), except that the counter quantization of the duty ratios is not modeled. The scripts therefore use a fine quantization in *motulator* (`CarrierComparison(N=2**24)`); with the default quantization of 4096 levels, the differences are about 1e-2. With single update (`single_update=True` and `delay=2` in *motulator*, i.e., the computational delay of one sampling period), the carrier frequency is `1/T_s`, and the delayed duty ratios change only at the maxima of the carrier, so the sampling also at its minima gives the single update.
- With the dead time of the converter (`t_d`, drives only), the Blanking Time block of PLECS delays the turn-on of the switches by `t_d`, and the converter is the IGBT converter of PLECS with ideal IGBTs and diodes: during blanking, both switches of the leg are off, and the current direction determines the conducting diode, as in *motulator* with `sign=np.sign`. With the threshold voltage of the power devices (`u_th`, drives only), the converter is the IGBT converter with parasitics of PLECS, whose forward voltages of the IGBTs and the diodes are `converter.u_th` (without on-resistances), so that the conducting device reduces the phase voltage by `u_th*sign(i)`, as in *motulator*.
- The duty-ratio error model of the PWM of the control system (`d_err = InverterNonlinearity(t_d, u_th, sign)`) is given by the dead time `pwm.t_d`, the threshold voltage `pwm.u_th`, and the current scale `pwm.i_0` of the initialization commands (no error model if `t_d = u_th = 0`), and the compensation can be disabled (`pwm.feedforward = 0`). The current direction in the control system is `sign = np.sign` (`i_0 = 0`, by default in the examples) or the smooth `sign = tanh(i/i_0)`, which avoids the chattering of the compensation due to the current ripple and the measurement noise near the zero crossings of the currents; `i_0` can be chosen to be about half the peak-to-peak current ripple. The minimum duty ratio of a switching leg (`PWM(d_min=...)`), the single update (`PWM(single_update=True)`, which halves the half carrier period of the error model), and the overmodulation (`'MPE'`, or `'MME'` as in V/Hz control) are also parameters of the PWM (`pwm.d_min`, `pwm.single_update`, and `pwm.overmodulation`).
- The synchronous machine is the PLECS permanent-magnet synchronous machine (`SynchronousMachinePars`) with the voltage-behind-reactance model (its rotor-reference-frame model, with controlled current sources, would toggle the diodes of a blocked leg at the zero crossings of its current) or a subsystem that looks like it (`SpatialSaturatedSynchronousMachinePars` with a GradNet current map with spatial harmonics): inside, a C-Script block computes the currents and the torque from the flux linkage, and they are injected with controlled current and torque sources.
- The induction machine is the PLECS squirrel-cage induction machine, parametrized as the T model with zero rotor leakage inductance, which is identical to the inverse-Γ model of *motulator*.
- The initial speed and angle of the machine (or of the inertia, and the angle sensor of the GradNet machine) are those of the mechanics of *motulator* (`mechanics.w_M0` and `mechanics.theta_M0` of the initialization commands).
- With the external rotor speed (`ExternalRotorSpeed`), a Controlled Rotational Speed source imposes the speed (the Step or Constant block `w_M ext.`) on the flange of the machine, whose moment of inertia is zero (the GradNet machine without the inertia), and the initial angle is that of the mechanics.
- The DC bus is a DC voltage source (without a DC-bus model) or a capacitor (`DCBus`), which can be fed from a diode bridge, supplied from the grid through an L filter (a composite model of a `GridConverterSystem` with `DiodeBridge`, `LFilter`, and `ThreePhaseSource`, and the drive; `grid.*` in the initialization commands). The DC-bus model feeds only the converter of the drive, and its capacitor voltage starts from the state of the model.
- The AC filter of the grid converter is an LCL filter or an L filter with its series resistance and the grid inductance (`LFilter`), with the grid as three AC voltage sources.
- The circuits are not grounded: as in the space-vector models of *motulator*, there is no zero-sequence path (grounding both the DC bus and the grid neutral would let the common-mode voltage of the converter drive a zero-sequence current).
- Voltmeters and ammeters are used only for the signals fed back to the control system. The AC voltages are measured line to line (u_ab and u_bc), as in practice. The other signals, e.g., the inductor currents shown in the scope, are measured with PLECS probes.
- A GradNet flux map of the control system is given as a struct in the initialization commands (`est_flux_map`).
- For `simulate`, a model written with `outputs=True` has the output ports `mdl` (the signals of the system model) and `ctrl` (the monitored signals), through which the RPC interface returns the signals.

## Library

The library `motulator_lib.plecs` has the blocks of the control systems (`CONTROL_BLOCKS` of `_library.py`, also the control systems of the Simulink library) and the GradNet machine, as the self-contained models have them, with the mask values of the model initialization (e.g., `vector_ctrl`). It is part of the package, and `tests/test_library.py` checks that it is up to date; rewrite it with `python -m motulator_export.plecs._library` after a change in the netlists or the writers.

- The C-Script blocks of the library include the C sources by paths relative to the library (`../c`). PLECS resolves them relative to the library also when the blocks are used in a model in another folder, as links from the Library Browser (`Type Reference`, `SrcComponent "motulator_lib/<block>"`, found on the library search path) or as Model References.
- With `library=True`, the model refers to the blocks by Model References (`Type ModelReference`, `SrcComponent "./<block>"`, and `SrcModel`, the path of the library relative to the model), which need no settings of PLECS; the component in the model gives the frame, the terminals, and the mask values (`_add_reference` of `_common.py`). PLECS replaces it with a copy of the block when it loads the model and synchronizes it before each simulation. The results equal those of the self-contained models exactly.
- The Library Browser shows the mask types of the blocks, so the mask types of the control systems are their names (e.g., `Flux-vector control (motulator)`), and the descriptions name the classes.

The differences are in SI units; relative to the signal magnitudes, they are about 1e-6 or below (e.g., 1e-3 W of 10 kW).
In `pmsyrm_6kw_gn_fvc.py`, the differences are larger (about 3e-6 relative to the torque and the current); they decrease with the tolerances of *motulator* (w_M 5.4e-6, tau_M 3.8e-5, and i_s_ab 1.3e-5 with 1e-10), but not with those of PLECS.
In `ipmsm_2kw_fvc.py --diode`, the diode bridge, supplied through the AC chokes, agrees at the level of the drive with a stiff DC bus (*motulator* locates the transitions of the diode states as events).
In `ipmsm_2kw_fvc.py --blocked`, the differences of the speed and the references are at the level of the drive without the gate blocking, which verifies the standby of the control system, the flying start after the release, and the blocked converter, whose diodes conduct the decaying currents.
The differences of the current and the torque are larger, at the level of the tolerance of 1e-4 A of the transitions of the diode states in *motulator*: a diode turns off when its current has reversed by the tolerance, whereas the ideal diodes of PLECS turn off at zero current.
In `ipmsm_2kw_cvc.py --blocked`, the flying start of current-vector control (the current reference and the current controller restarting from the zero-torque operating point of the caught speed) agrees as closely.

### Dead time

In `im_2kw_dead_time_cvc.py`, the differences are due to the switching transients during blanking and at the zero crossings of the currents, which *motulator* does not model in detail.
*motulator* determines the state of a blanked leg and the voltage drop over the threshold voltage from the current direction at the latest switching instant, whereas in PLECS, the current of a blanked leg cannot cross zero, since its diode turns off, and the current of a leg stays at zero while the voltage across its devices is below their forward voltage.
At low speeds, this occurs often due to the current ripple near the zero crossings of the phase currents, and the sensorless low-speed operation amplifies the differences (e.g., 2.5 rad/s in the speed estimate during transients, compared with the speed reference of 7.9 rad/s).
Furthermore, at the first switching from zero currents, the blanked leg is open in PLECS.

The dead time causes most of the differences (w_M 0.77 and ctrl.w_M 2.4 with `U_TH = 1e-9`), and the single update, i.e., the twice higher switching frequency, makes them larger than with double update (w_M 0.23 and ctrl.w_M 1.3 with *motulator* 0.12.0, without the threshold voltage).
With `T_D = 1e-9` and `U_TH = 1e-9` in the script, the differences are at the level of the other examples (e.g., w_M 1.4e-6, i_s_ab 8.6e-6, ctrl.w_M 3.1e-6), which verifies the export of the dead time, the threshold voltage, the single update, and their compensation; with `T_D = 1e-9` and `U_TH = 1.0`, they are w_M 6.0e-3, i_s_ab 2.0e-2, and ctrl.w_M 6.2e-2.
With `--tanh` (`I_0 = 0.05` A, about half the peak-to-peak current ripple, which the single update halves from the 0.15–0.28 A of double update), the compensation is less sensitive to these differences near the zero crossings: the differences are smaller than with the signum function (e.g., w_M 0.36 and ctrl.w_M 2.0).
